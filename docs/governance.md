# Governance Model

Narayan Labs operates on an open, community-driven governance structure. This document outlines the hierarchy of roles, responsibilities, and decision-making processes.

## Roles

### Core Maintainers
- **Responsibility**: Strategic direction, final architectural decisions, release management, and enforcing the Code of Conduct.
- **Selection**: Core maintainers are nominated from the Research Fellows based on consistent, high-impact contributions and community leadership.

### Research Fellows
- **Responsibility**: Leading specific research programs (e.g., Legal AI, Agriculture AI), mentoring contributors, and coordinating major roadmap milestones.
- **Selection**: Appointed through our Fellowship Program or nominated based on sustained, specialized contributions.

### Contributors
- **Responsibility**: Submitting code, creating datasets, writing documentation, and reporting bugs.
- **Selection**: Anyone who has successfully merged a pull request or made a significant non-code contribution (e.g., dataset curation).

### Community Members
- **Responsibility**: Participating in discussions, voting on RFCs (Request for Comments), and testing alpha/beta models.
- **Selection**: Anyone who joins our forums, Discord, or GitHub discussions.

## Decision Making and Review Process
1. **RFC Process**: Major architectural changes or new research agendas must begin as an RFC issue. The community discusses the proposal for a minimum of 7 days.
2. **Consensus Building**: We strive for lazy consensus. If there are strong objections, a vote among Research Fellows and Maintainers is held.
3. **Pull Request Review**: All PRs require at least one approving review from a Research Fellow or Maintainer. Core architectural changes require two.
4. **Releases**: Final approval for model releases and dataset publications rests with the Core maintainers, ensuring quality and safety standards are met.
